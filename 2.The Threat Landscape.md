## **1. The Threat Landscape & Terminology**

This section outlines the broad categories of risks organizations face and the specific language used to describe them.

- **12 Categories of Threat:**
- **Intellectual Property Compromises:** Includes piracy and copyright infringement.

- **Quality of Service Deviations:** Issues with ISPs, power, or WAN services.

- **Espionage/Trespass:** Unauthorized access or data collection.

- **Forces of Nature:** Fire, floods, earthquakes, lightning.

- **Human Error:** Accidents and employee mistakes.

- **Information Extortion:** Blackmail or threat of disclosure.

- **Sabotage/Vandalism:** Destruction of systems or information.

- **Software Attacks:** Viruses, worms, macros, and Denial of Service (DoS).

- **Technical Hardware Failures:** Equipment errors or failure.

- **Technical Software Failures:** Bugs, code problems, and loopholes.

- **Technological Obsolescence:** Risks from using antiquated technologies.

- **Theft:** Illegal confiscation of equipment or information.

- **Key Terminology:**
- **Malware:** A general term for any software intentionally designed to disrupt operations, gain unauthorized access, steal info, or cause damage.

- **Vulnerability:** A weakness in a system (software, hardware, or procedure) that can be exploited.
- **Exploit:** The method or code used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

- **Zero-Day:** A vulnerability that is unknown to the vendor or developer, meaning no patch exists yet (**Additional Knowledge**).

## **2. Employee and Ex-Employee Threats**

Employees are often cited as the "weakest link" in security because they have legitimate access to systems.

- **Nature of the Threat:**
- Employees and ex-employees are a major source of computer crimes.

- Managing personnel with access is a critical security priority.

- **Types of Employee Threats:**
- **Unintentional:**
- Forgetting security rules or failing to follow procedures.

- Creating vulnerabilities unknowingly (e.g., weak passwords, clicking links).

- **Intentional:**
- Knowingly violating controls or assisting in security breaches.

- Specific acts include unauthorized access, altering/deleting data, holding data hostage, or stealing strategic data (espionage) .

- **Mitigation Strategies:**
- **Background Checks & Screening:**
- Verify resumes (education/employment history) and check for discrepancies.

- Conduct criminal record and credit checks (to assess financial stress risks).

- **Employment Agreements:**
- Contracts must include non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) and explicit reference to security policies.

- Employees must review and agree to abide by these policies.

- **Security Principles During Employment:**
- **Least Privilege:** Give minimal access required for the job.

- **Separation of Duties:** Split critical tasks to avoid conflicts of interest.

- **Redundancy:** Avoid over-reliance on single key employees.

- **Termination Procedures:**
- Based on ISO 27002, the goal is an orderly exit and removal of access.

- **Key Actions:**
- Remove from access lists and revoke personal access codes/cards.

- Change locks and reprogram systems.

- Recover company assets (IDs, devices, documents) immediately.

## **3. Hackers and Attacks**

This section covers the actors behind external threats and the technical methods they use.

- **Hacker Profiles:**
- **Expert Hackers:** Develop their own software scripts and exploits; they are masters of many skills.

- **Unskilled Hackers (Script Kiddies):** Use expertly written software to exploit systems without fully understanding how they work.

- **Cracker:** Removes software protection to prevent unauthorized duplication.

- **Phreaker:** Hacks public telephone systems for free calls.

- **Specific Attack Types:**
- **Denial-of-Service (DoS):**
- A hacker floods a target computer with more requests than it can handle to disrupt service.

- **Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS):**
- Uses a "zombie army" or botnet (hundreds of compromised computers) to conduct a coordinated flooding attack.

- **IP Spoofing:**
- The attacker modifies the source IP address of a packet to fool a firewall into thinking the traffic is legitimate .

- **Man-in-the-Middle (MITM):**
- An attacker intercepts transmission between two parties (e.g., Company A and B), exchanges their own keys, and decrypts/modifies messages without the victims knowing.

- **Password Attacks:**
- **Brute Force:** Trying every possible combination of characters (**Additional Knowledge**).
- **Dictionary Attack:** Using a list of common words or passwords.

- **Rainbow Tables:** Using pre-computed hash tables to reverse encrypted passwords.

- **Sniffing/Shoulder Surfing:**
- **Sniffing:** Monitoring network traffic to capture data (**Additional Knowledge**).
- **Shoulder Surfing:** Observing someone entering confidential info (e.g., passwords) physically.

## **4. The Criminal Era Competitor Threats**

This refers to "Corporate Espionage," where businesses attack rivals to gain an unfair advantage.

- **Nature and Motivation:**
- Involves theft of trade secrets, intellectual property, and customer databases.

- **Motivations:** Gaining market advantage, disrupting rival operations, and copying innovations without paying for R&D.

- **Common Techniques:**
- Hacking rival networks.

- Planting insiders or bribing employees.

- Reverse engineering products.

- Spreading misinformation/fake news.

- **Real-World Examples:**
- **Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi (2006):** Pepsi employees were caught attempting to steal Coke's trade secrets.

- **Google vs. Uber (2017):** A high-profile lawsuit over stolen self-driving car technology.

- **Defense Strategies:**
- Implement strong policies and monitoring.

- Use encryption for communication and data.

- Protect intellectual property and ensure confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of corporate data.

## **5. Cyberwar and Cyberterrorism**

These threats move beyond financial gain to political and military objectives.

- **Cyberwar:**
- **Definition:** The use of computer technology to disrupt the activities of a state or organization, specifically attacking info systems for strategic/military purposes.

- **Targets:** Critical computing systems, communication networks, power infrastructures .

- **Cyberterrorism:**
- **Definition (FBI):** Premeditated, politically motivated attacks against information systems that result in violence against noncombatant targets.

- **Origin:** The term was coined in the 1980s by Barry Collin.

- **Key Example (2002 DNS Attack):**
- A DDoS attack targeted the 13 root DNS servers on Oct 21, 2002.

- 9 out of 13 servers were jammed, though only 6% of requests failed at the peak.

- It highlighted the vulnerability of global infrastructure.

## **6. Malware and Social Engineering**

Malware is the tool; social engineering is often the delivery method.

- **Malware Types:**
- **Virus:** Attaches to a legitimate file and requires human action (e.g., running a program) to spread.

- **Worm:** Spreads automatically through networks without human action. It consumes resources and propagates rapidly.

- **Trojan Horse:** Disguises itself as legitimate software to trick users. It does not self-replicate but creates backdoors.

- **Ransomware:** Encrypts victim files and demands payment for the decryption key (e.g., WannaCry in 2017).

- **Spyware:** Secretly monitors user activity (keystrokes, browsing) to gather credentials.

- **Adware:** Automatically displays unwanted ads, often bundled with free software.

- **Rootkit:** Hides deep within the OS to conceal itself and other malware, providing persistent privileged access.

- **Keyloggers:** Hardware or software that records every keystroke.

- **Logic Bombs:** Code embedded in legitimate programs that activates when specific conditions are met (e.g., a specific date or when an employee is fired) .

- **Bots and Botnets:**
- **Bot (Zombie):** A compromised device controlled remotely by an attacker.

- **Botnet:** A network of bots controlled by a "botmaster," used for DDoS attacks and spam.

- **Social Engineering:**
- **Definition:** Using social skills/trickery to convince people to reveal access credentials or other valuable information.

- **Phishing:** Sending emails with malicious attachments or links to trick users.

- **Drive-by Downloads:** Malware installed simply by visiting a compromised website.

- **Malware Analysis & Detection:**
- **Static/Signature-Based:** Identifies malware using unique code signatures/identifiers.

- **Dynamic/Behavioral:** Analyzes how the code behaves (file system activities, network comms) often using a **Sandbox** (isolated virtual environment).

- **Incident Response Cycle:** Preparation Identification Containment Eradication Recovery Lessons Learned .
